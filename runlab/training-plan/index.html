FILLING IN YOUR DETAILS

Target race: The event distance you're preparing for: 5K, 10K, half marathon or marathon. Not your current longest run.

Race date: The actual event day. RunLab checks whether the remaining weeks are likely enough, taking your present running into account.

Recent race / benchmark: Choose 3K, 5K or 10K and enter your actual recent finishing time (ideally within the last 4–8 weeks). Choose NONE if you haven't timed a run; you'll train by effort.

Target time: Optional finish-time goal. Workout paces are based on demonstrated fitness, not an ambitious goal.

Runs per week: The number of days you can consistently train. RunLab may prescribe fewer runs if your recent weekly distance is too low; rest days matter.

Preferred run days (optional): Choose exactly the same number of weekdays as Runs Per Week, or leave every day unchecked and RunLab will suggest a schedule. Race week is arranged around your actual race date.

KM per week: How far you currently run in a typical week—not your target. Example: 2 km + 3 km + 5 km = 10 km/week.

Longest recent run: The farthest single running session completed in the past four weeks. If your latest run was 2 km but you ran 5 km two weeks ago, enter 5 km. Include walking breaks within that session.

READING YOUR PLAN

Weekly volume is the sum of all your scheduled sessions, not one run. For example: 3 km + 3 km + 4 km = 10 km in total.

RUN 1 / RUN 2 / RUN 3 are the order of your workouts. Suggested weekdays can be moved, but keep recovery between harder and longer sessions.

Easy aerobic / long easy: Jog at a pace where you can speak in sentences. A long run is relative to your experience; for a beginner it may be only 2 km.

Run/walk: Follow the jogging and walking intervals. Take longer walking breaks when needed. No fast running required.

Tempo / intervals: These appear only when the input data indicates a stronger base. Follow the warm-up, work, recovery and cooldown exactly as shown.

Pace ranges: Guidance in minutes per kilometre from your recent benchmark, not compulsory speeds. Heat, hills and tiredness take priority over the watch.

Too-short training window: A conservative warning, not a medical diagnosis. If the window is too short, RunLab won't schedule a full race-distance attempt. Consider a later event or shorter distance.
