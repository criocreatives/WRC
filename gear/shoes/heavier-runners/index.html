WHAT COUNTS AS A HEAVIER RUNNER?

There is no universal cutoff. For this guide, roughly 85 kg / 187 lb and above is our editorial starting point. Other guides use 90 kg / 198 lb, and research uses different groupings. This is a shoe-selection audience, not a medical classification or a judgment about fitness. Muscle contributes to body mass too.

Body weight does not tell the whole story: stride, pace, foot shape, running surface and your preference for softness or support all matter. Being heavier does not automatically mean you need a stability shoe or the softest foam.

CHOOSE THE RIDE YOU CAN USE FOR THE WHOLE RUN

For long runs and half-marathon training, start by trying the Superblast 3. For a firmer, more controlled easy-day feel, compare the Glycerin Max 2. If you prefer guidance, try the Hurricane 26 before the livelier neutral options. The best shoe is the one that stays comfortable and controlled for your feet at your usual pace.

More stack height or a higher price does not guarantee more useful cushioning for you. A flat or harsh sensation on a run can have several causes, and mileage alone cannot confirm that a midsole is worn out. Track changes in cushioning feel, uneven wear and how securely the shoe holds your foot; avoid treating any kilometre figure as a universal replacement deadline.
